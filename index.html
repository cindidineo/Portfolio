Chain-of-Thought + Persona Prompting

How prompting technique impacts AI output — with a budget example and step-by-step calculations

Overview

This page demonstrates how adding a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) instruction and a persona (e.g., "senior business analyst") to an AI prompt yields clearer, verifiable, and more useful outputs for business decisions.

Below we also take a real budget example from your note and present improved text, clear assumptions, step-by-step calculations, and recommendations.

Problem statement

A company has an R120,000 budget. Two staffing options are proposed for a project that lasts 3 months:

  • Option A: Hire 2 junior developers at R25,000 each per month.
  • Option B: Hire 1 senior developer at R50,000 per month and spend R20,000 on AI tools (one-time).

Question: Which option fits the budget and which should be recommended?

Important: The original prompt is ambiguous about whether the R120,000 budget is a total budget for the entire project or a monthly budget. Both interpretations are analyzed below.

Analysis & calculations

Assumption 1 — Budget = R120,000 total for the entire 3-month project

Compute total cost of each option over 3 months.

Option A (2 juniors)

  1. Monthly cost per junior = R25,000
  2. Two juniors monthly cost = 2 × R25,000 = R50,000 per month
  3. Total for 3 months = R50,000 × 3 = R150,000
  4. Compare to budget: R150,000 > R120,000 → Exceeds budget by R30,000

Option B (1 senior + AI tools)

  1. Senior monthly cost = R50,000
  2. Cost for 3 months = R50,000 × 3 = R150,000
  3. One-time AI tools = R20,000
  4. Total cost = R150,000 + R20,000 = R170,000
  5. Compare to budget: R170,000 > R120,000 → Exceeds budget by R50,000

Conclusion under Assumption 1: Neither option fits a total budget of R120,000. Option A costs R150,000 and Option B costs R170,000.

Assumption 2 — Budget = R120,000 per month (i.e., monthly runway)

Compute monthly and 3-month totals:

Option A (2 juniors)

  1. Two juniors monthly cost = R50,000
  2. Monthly budget R120,000 > R50,000 → Affordable per month
  3. Total for 3 months = R150,000 < total available across 3 months (R360,000)

Option B (1 senior + AI tools)

  1. Senior monthly cost = R50,000
  2. One-time AI tools = R20,000
  3. Monthly cost is R50,000 (affordable), and the AI tools are a small one-time spend
  4. Total for 3 months = R170,000 < total available across 3 months (R360,000)

Conclusion under Assumption 2: Both options are affordable if the R120,000 is a monthly budget; the company can easily fund either option.

Recommendation

Because the original input did not state whether R120,000 is total or per-month, the most important first step is to clarify that point. After clarifying, choose based on these trade-offs:

  • If R120,000 is total for 3 months: Neither option is currently feasible. Consider alternatives:
    • Negotiate shorter contract duration or lower monthly rates.
    • Hire a single junior (R25,000/month) or a contractor for part-time work.
    • Re-scope the project or phase it to fit the R120,000 limit.
  • If R120,000 is monthly: Both options are affordable. Choose based on expected outcomes:
    • Option A (2 juniors) may allow more developer throughput but could require stronger senior oversight.
    • Option B (senior + AI tools) could yield higher-quality design and faster delivery per developer-day, and AI tools might boost individual productivity. If the project needs complex architecture or leadership, prefer Option B.

Practical suggestion: Clarify budget timing (total vs monthly). If total, re-budget or consider a phased approach; if monthly, prefer Option B when you need senior leadership and quality, otherwise Option A for parallel throughput and lower single-person risk.

Why Chain-of-Thought + Persona helps

The plain prompt produced a short, vague answer. Adding a CoT instruction and a persona (e.g., "You are a senior business analyst and financial consultant. Think step-by-step before answering.") forces the model to:

  • Make assumptions explicit (or ask for clarifications).
  • Show intermediate calculations so the user can verify them.
  • Provide structured reasoning and a clear recommendation that aligns with business judgment.

In practice, always include instructions if you want the model to show work: e.g., "Show your calculations and reasoning clearly" and mention the persona if you want domain-appropriate framing.
